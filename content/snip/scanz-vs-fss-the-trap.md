# SCANZ Springs the Trap on Frontier Security Services

Star Citizen Australia & New Zealand, better known as SCANZ, set the net, FSS took the bait, and what followed was a clean little reminder that “weak” is a very dangerous thing to mistake us for.

---

## Mission Brief

* Objective: Establish interdiction points, pull targets, and create trouble on purpose
* Location: Stanton Gateway to Terminus, then Stanton Gateway to Levski
* Ships involved: Two crewed Perseus gunships, fighter support, and assorted SCANZ chaos assets
* Outcome: Frontier Security Services baited, FSS broken, SCANZ held the field

---

## Star Citizen Interdiction in Nyx

Tuesday’s operation began with SCANZ setting up multiple interdiction points across the verse, backed by two manned Perseus gunships and a fighter screen.

The first stop was the Stanton Gateway to Terminus route in Pyro. It was quiet. Very quiet. The sort of quiet that makes you wonder if everyone suddenly developed survival instincts.

They had not.

After the initial route dried up, SCANZ shifted operations into Nyx, setting up along the Stanton Gateway to Levski line. That is where the net started catching traffic. We pulled several ships, got a few captures, and enjoyed the usual mix of confusion, panic, and poor life choices.

Then came the good one.

## Frontier Security Services Takes the Bait

A few nights earlier, SCANZ had invited Frontier Security Services, officially known as FROSS and often referred to as FSS, to come fight us in fighters at Checkmate.

FSS responded by flying three Idrises over from Stanton, which was less “fighter fight” and more “someone failed to read the room with capital ship confidence.”

SCANZ took one look at that and made the tactical decision to leave them to it.

FSS, in their role as supposed Frontier Security Services, then apparently spent their evening shooting other people at Checkmate.

So when FSS appeared at our interdiction point with a handful of fighters, it felt less like coincidence and more like a very tidy result from a well-placed hook.

This time, there were no Idrises to hide behind.

The fight started quickly, and SCANZ began whittling FSS down piece by piece. Their leader, FSS-Bavar, was the first major loss of the engagement, going down early and setting the tone for the rest of the encounter.

Reports from the field suggest he was not especially thrilled about this development.

From there, SCANZ cleaned up the remaining FSS presence and held control of the area. The trap had worked. The response had been drawn in. The result was decisive.

FSS came looking for SCANZ.

They found us.

## Repairs, Global Chat, and One Very Emotional Flight Path

After the fight, SCANZ moved to Levski to repair and rearm. GFs were thrown into chat. Some came back.

From Bavar, silence.

Shortly after we landed, several FSS ships pulled up and parked themselves nearby on the pad. What followed was not another grand military engagement, but something far more SCANZ: twenty minutes of absolute rubbish in global chat.

Diplomacy was attempted.

By which we mean everyone talked shite.

Still nothing from Bavar.

Then, as Skidds lifted off from Levski, Bavar followed close behind. With Levski protected by an armistice zone and weapons no longer an option, Bavar’s ship made a bold and deeply emotional commitment to occupying the same airspace as Skidds. Officially, it was an “accidental collision.” Unofficially, the timing, flight path, and general vibe were doing a lot of talking.

Bavar was not seen again.

## Highlights

* SCANZ shifted from a dry Pyro interdiction route into Nyx and found much better content
* Frontier Security Services arrived exactly where we wanted them, without three Idrises this time
* FSS-Bavar went down early, which may have affected morale, decision-making, and/or throttle discipline
* Levski global chat became the true endgame
* The “this should not have worked but did” moment: baiting FSS into a fighter engagement after they previously tried to answer a fighter fight with three Idrises

---

## Lessons Learned

* If you bring three Idrises to a fighter fight, people will remember
* Armistice zones prevent weapons, not feelings
* “Weak” Star Citizen orgs apparently require multiple FSS ships to deal with
* Never underestimate SCANZ’s ability to turn repairs into content
* Physics remains the most dangerous weapon in Star Citizen

## Final Thoughts

Tuesday’s interdiction was exactly the kind of operation SCANZ does best: a bit planned, a bit improvised, and somehow ending with wreckage, laughter, and someone very loudly not typing in global.

For anyone looking for a Star Citizen Australia & New Zealand community that enjoys organised chaos, PvP, industry, events, and the occasional questionable flight path, this is very much the house style.

Frontier Security Services came in thinking they were dealing with a weak org.

They left with fewer ships, fewer answers, and one very awkward Levski incident.

SCANZ victory. FSS baited. Bavar tilted. Global chat fed.

A productive Tuesday, really.
